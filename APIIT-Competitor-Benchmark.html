The short version

Each rival beats APIIT on a different front, so the answer is a set of targeted fixes, not a rebuild.

IIT leads on industry links and AI: five AI degrees, a paid 48-week internship and a Career Day with 80+ organisations. ICBT leads on reach, with 10 campuses, and sells a UK AI route for about LKR 1.4 million. CINEC leads on campus scale, facilities and funding, and its Anglia Ruskin University partnership now puts UK degrees in computing, AI and engineering on that campus. APIIT stands out for University of Staffordshire degrees in six subject areas, Law included, a 25-year UK partnership, its Extravaganza and ALSINC events, and the longest list of industry certifications. Its UK partner was named the Daily Mail’s Modern University of the Year 2027 in August, a ready-made headline APIIT has not yet used.

The biggest gap is cost and funding. ICBT’s and CINEC’s own degrees are listed at up to LKR 1.5 million under the government’s interest-free loan, which pays that fee. CINEC also publishes its UK-degree fees and lets students spread them over 30 monthly payments. APIIT’s degrees cost about LKR 2.93 million (LKR 960,000 a year plus LKR 50,000 registration), paid while studying; its UK awards do not qualify for the loan, and apiit.lk publishes no fees.

In AI, APIIT sits in the middle on price: LKR 2.93 million, against about LKR 4 million plus registration quoted by IIT, about LKR 2.49 million at CINEC for its 2027 intake and LKR 1.37 million for ICBT’s UK route; APIIT’s and CINEC’s figures are before a 2.5% government tax. Its module list misses the newer topics IIT and ICBT now teach: generative AI and large language models, agentic AI and MLOps. Its lab PCs also have built-in graphics, not the GPUs promised at launch.

Do these five first

Four cost little and can start this term; the fifth needs a management decision now.

  1. Fix the website’s numbers and claimsOne set of figures, one university name, 2026 news on the homepage, and a sourced headline: Staffordshire’s Daily Mail award is ready to use.
  2. Publish fees, the help on offer and a scholarship ladderCINEC shows fees on its course pages and spreads them over 30 monthly payments. APIIT publishes none, not even its 20% sibling discount or its monthly payments at no extra cost, and the only listing online is out of date. At LKR 960,000 a year, a scholarship ladder and a longer instalment option answer IIT and CINEC directly.
  3. Add WhatsApp and student-ambassador chatIIT and ICBT make it easy to ask a question from the website; apiit.lk does not yet.
  4. Brief counsellors and mystery-shop the rivalsUse the playbook in chapter 14, and keep phone-checking fees: IIT quoted about LKR 4 million plus registration for AI, but its other prices, and ICBT’s beyond AI, are still unknown.
  5. Take a local degree route to managementIt is the only way to open the interest-free loan to APIIT students. ICBT and CINEC already have their own degrees and IIT has just launched one.

Who can apply

  • Sat A/Ls in 2023, 2024 or 2025, with three S passes in one sitting
  • An S pass in General English at A/L, or in English at O/L
  • A Common General Test score of 30 or more
  • Aged 25 or under
  • To stay on the loan: 80% attendance and at least a C in every compulsory subject
